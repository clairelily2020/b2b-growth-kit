# Search patterns

Use public professional sources and respect each site's terms. Search engine operators are a starting point for research, not a scraping pipeline.

## Search operator patterns

Basic structure:

```text
site:linkedin.com/in/ "{role}" "{industry or keyword}" "{region}"
```

Combine synonyms with OR and group with parentheses:

```text
site:linkedin.com/in/ ("trade show" OR "exhibition" OR "events") ("Marketing Manager" OR "Event Manager") ("United States" OR "USA")
```

Company pages:

```text
site:linkedin.com/company/ "{category}" "{region}"
```

By seniority (replace the keyword with your ICP's domain):

```text
site:linkedin.com/in/ "{keyword}" ("VP" OR "Vice President" OR "Head of")
site:linkedin.com/in/ "{keyword}" ("Director" OR "Senior Manager")
```

By industry, swap the industry phrase: "manufacturing", "medical device", "software", "industrial automation".

Tips:
- Exact phrases in quotes; avoid stacking too many terms or results vanish.
- Titles vary; search the function ("demand generation", "field marketing") as well as titles.
- Results show public snippets only; verify the person's current role before using it.

## Event and exhibitor sources

1. Official exhibitor directory or downloadable list (highest confidence).
2. Official floor plan or exhibitor map (candidate discovery; needs a second source).
3. Event press releases, sponsor pages and speaker pages.
4. Company newsroom posts announcing participation.
5. Third-party event databases (medium confidence; check dates).

## Contact path discovery

- Company team, leadership or press pages.
- Public speaker bios and podcast guest pages.
- The company's general contact or partnership routes for account-level outreach.
- Commercial databases only when their terms and your lawful basis allow it.

## Three-step validation for contacts

1. Role match: title and seniority match the profile's buying roles.
2. Company match: the company is verified and in tier A or B.
3. Reachability: a verified work email or a legitimate platform route exists. Bounce-test emails with a verification tool before any sequence; keep the bounce rate under 3%.
