# S0: Prove someone needs this job done

**When:** you have an idea, or even a product, but cannot say who would pay for it.

The goal is to find a specific, recurring job that someone is accountable for. Not "every B2B company needs efficiency", but who, at what moment, because of what, has to deliver what result.

## How to run this round

1. **Start with one segment you can reach.** Record industry, company size, region, role, current method and the most recent trigger. Size is a filter; the buying job is what you study.
2. **Talk to people who do the job today** about their last real instance. Ask them to show the spreadsheet, process or deliverable rather than rate your idea.
3. **Also interview people who did not buy or gave up on a trial.** Listening only to enthusiasts turns politeness and survivorship bias into "demand".
4. **Deliver the result once by hand or with a small sample.** Write down scope, timing, inputs, price and what happens if it fails. Do not build a full platform for a repeat need that has not appeared yet.
5. **Record what they are willing to give:** time, data, a referral, a pilot budget, a procurement step. "Interested" stays in the interest column.

Start with about five interviews to fix your questions, then decide whether to run 10-15 more. That is a workload plan, not proof of demand once the count is reached.

## What to ask

- When did you last do this? Why did it have to happen that week?
- Who does it now, with what tools, and who receives the result?
- Which step is slowest, and what happens when it goes wrong?
- What have you tried instead? Why do you still use it?
- If a scoped result were offered, who would evaluate it, who would pay, and who might object?
- What would make you not buy? What would make a pilot fail?

Ask less "would you use this?". A promise to pay on the spot still waits for an actual transaction before it counts as a sale.

## What to prepare

The [interview template](../templates/customer-interview.md), a short service description, a sample you can show, and a follow-up tracker. The website only needs to explain who it is for, the job, a sample and how to get in touch. Dozens of SEO pages or a full brand system can wait.

## When to move to S1

Different buyers describe a similar job, can name what the current approach costs them, and at least some show real pilot intent with an executable next step. Compliments from friends, free trials or "when we have budget" are weak evidence.

If every request needs heavy customization, decide whether you are building a services business or software. Services can be the right answer, but price and cost them as services.

**Example:** "we need trade show data" splits into choosing which shows to attend, researching exhibitors, finding sales targets and screening suppliers. They use similar data but have different buyers, promises and ways to close. Interview for each job separately; similar data does not mean a validated market.
