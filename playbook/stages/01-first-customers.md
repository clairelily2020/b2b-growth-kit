# S1: Win the first customers and deliver what they bought

**When:** you have a specific buying job and a product that can deliver it, but payments still come by chance.

"The first ten customers" is a working scope, not a product-market-fit certificate. What matters is knowing why each one came, why they bought, and whether they got the result they expected.

## Who to reach first

List accounts where you can explain the fit. Twenty to thirty is a sensible manual research scope, not a sending quota. Sources: past working relationships, referrals from interviews, public buying signals, niche communities, industry events and existing trial users.

For each account write one sentence: what they do, what suggests they may need this now, and which step your product helps with. Exhibiting at an event or hiring is a signal, not proof of intent.

Book conversations through warm introductions, founder outreach and a small amount of targeted outbound. Low-priced self-serve tools can let people try directly; complex purchases get a needs check and a task-based demo. Do not force everyone into a demo.

## How an early deal comes together

1. Describe their current situation using their real task, before showing every feature.
2. Show the result you can deliver and what it does not cover.
3. Offer a scoped paid pilot: quantity, quality bar, inputs, timing, and what happens if it falls short.
4. Confirm the user, the budget owner, the procurement steps and the evaluation date.
5. After delivery, ask whether the result was usable; record rework, support time and direct cost.
6. Propose the next natural purchase (the next event, the next set of accounts, the next period). Do not hide low usage behind an annual discount.

## Build the minimum trust at the same time

The site shows the real team, real product screens, sample sources and update dates. Without customer results, use a demo case clearly labelled as simulated. A payment from a large-company email address does not mean group-wide procurement, or permission to show their logo.

Prepare the [paid pilot template](../templates/paid-pilot.md), a [demo outline](../templates/demo.md) and a [proof card](../templates/proof-card.md). Be clear about payment terms, contract and invoicing entity, and the service contact.

## What to review

Per account: source, buying job, quote, reasons for any no, payment date, first value, delivery cost and next step. Keep a low-friction feedback channel for self-serve customers; do not keep chasing people who declined an interview.

Move to [S2](02-repeatability.md) when the same kind of need repeats, the offer gets easier to explain, and rework stays under control. If every deal depends on the founder rescuing it, fix the product, packaging or delivery first.
