# S4: Scale when you can see the costs

**When:** the buying job, delivery, sources and repeatability have evidence, and you are about to add budget, hire or enter a new market.

Before scaling, separate unit economics from cash. Signing a one-year contract is not receiving a year of cash, and receiving cash does not remove the cost of delivering.

## Decision table

| What you want to scale | Check first | Pull back if |
|---|---|---|
| Ad budget | Deals from similar accounts, source completeness, cost in mature cohorts | Extra budget only buys low-quality traffic |
| Media mix | Each outlet's audience and downstream results, delivery capacity | The same readers are reached repeatedly with no increment |
| SDR headcount | List quality, messaging, reply handling, sales capacity to take meetings | Meetings rise but qualified opportunities do not |
| New region | Local language, procurement, payments, support and compliance | Value in the home region is not stable yet, or translation is mistaken for localization |
| New package | Usage frequency, service cost, margin and reasons to upgrade | Selling more makes manual service lose more money |

## Set limits from your own economics

Acquisition cost includes media, creators, tools and the relevant people's time. Keep direct channel cost and fully loaded cost apart. With zero paying customers, CAC is not zero; it cannot be computed yet.

Payback can be estimated as acquisition cost divided by monthly gross margin per customer. If margin and retention are not stable, use conservative, base and optimistic scenarios rather than a predicted lifetime value that excuses today's losses.

Arithmetic example only: a pilot costing $2,000 in total produced 2 new paying accounts in a mature cohort, so CAC on that definition is $1,000. With $200 gross margin per account per month, static payback is about 5 months, before churn, late payments and the cost of capital. This is not a recommended CAC benchmark.

## How to scale

Keep the validated audience and offer, increase one input at a time, and record when each change happened. New regions, new personas and new channels each get their own experiment; they do not count as copying the old model. When sales or support backs up, add capacity there first.

Hire around a persistent bottleneck: more customer conversations call for sales or research; better distribution calls for content and partnerships. Write the outputs, handoff standards and acceptance checks first, then the job title.

Each month, use the [review template](../templates/weekly-review.md) to check: is growth coming from new customers, higher prices or expansion; does it depend on one large customer; and how long the company could operate if the biggest channel stopped.
