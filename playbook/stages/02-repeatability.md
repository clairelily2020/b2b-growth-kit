# S2: Turn scattered deals into a path you can repeat

**When:** you have paying and partly active customers, but do not know where the next batch will come from.

Group customers by buying job first, then compare their sources. An event's industry is not the customer's industry, and a company's industry is not its use case. Do not infer a segment's conversion rate from three wins; the denominator includes everyone reached, signed up and lost.

## Find one path worth repeating

Use the [experiment card](../templates/experiment.md) to write: a type of account, at a moment, sees a promise in a channel, takes an action, and ends with a result. Record one path per experiment instead of mixing a quarter's activities into one.

Choose channels on four questions: are buyers there, do they have the need now, can you make a credible promise, and is someone ready to follow up. With a small team, one main acquisition effort plus one content distribution effort is a sensible capacity limit; it does not override approvals or parallel tests already agreed.

## How to run it

1. Build the matching page, sample and follow-up script for the target group.
2. Carry the source from the ad or partner link through signup, the CRM and payment; check it end to end before spending.
3. Decide in advance the measurement window, the maximum acceptable loss, what counts as a valid lead, and the stop rule.
4. Each week, check what changed: audience, promise, entry point, experience or sales follow-up. Without enough traffic, use interviews and session reviews instead of forcing A/B significance.
5. Keep customer cohorts by the week they entered from each channel and let the sales cycle play out. No revenue last week does not make a channel permanently useless.

## When to add investment

Sources can be verified; several independent accounts followed the same buying path; delivery and support costs are affordable; and there are more similar accounts to reach next round. The thresholds come from your deal size, margin, sales cycle and cash, not an industry average.

When attribution is broken, the conclusion is "we cannot see yet", not "the channel failed". Do not scale on unreliable conversions either. Brand content can continue; scaling decisions that depend on bad data pause.

See the [field notes](../cases/field-notes.md) for how a newsletter placement and a niche directory produced very different levels of evidence. One cannot stand in for the other.
