# Field notes from an early-stage B2B data product

Lessons from running marketing for a small B2B data product with a two-person marketing team. Names of the company, partners, vendors and customers are removed. These are practice records with their limits stated, not benchmarks.

## 1. A partnership going live is the first of several results

**What happened:** a niche newsletter placement produced published content and source-attributed signups within its observation window. There were signs of product usage, but not enough evidence to attribute new revenue to it. Not finding a payment event did not prove there was no offline deal either.

**Method:** record each layer separately: content published, link working, meaningful visits, signups, task completion, opportunities, payments. A link shortener is not a complete record of visits. Keep partnership costs in three states: quoted, committed, paid.

**Do not generalize:** one newsletter producing signups does not mean every B2B newsletter fits, and one small sample does not justify scaling.

## 2. Buying a listing and getting results are two separate acceptances

**What happened:** a paid niche directory listing was delivered (placement and assets confirmed), but visits from it were very low, and the check did not fully exclude test traffic. Status in different internal tracking sheets had drifted apart.

**Method:** first accept the actual placement, link, assets and benefits; then check buyer behaviour in your own data. Never estimate your listing's revenue from the outlet's total traffic.

**Do not generalize:** one weak listing does not prove directories never work, and it is no reason to make other media wait. Evaluate outlets item by item and in parallel.

## 3. Unknown attribution is not organic traffic

**What happened:** the analysis had to separate empty sources, internal tags and real external sources; measurement work then defined signup, booking, meeting held and payment separately.

**Method:** keep raw source values and the customer's self-reported source; map them in reports without overwriting the originals. Deduplicate on real business IDs, and check the link from the marketing site to the app and the CRM. When sources are thin, say you cannot see; do not hand unattributed revenue to your favourite channel.

**Do not generalize:** configuration done does not mean production data arrives; data arriving at an ad platform does not mean it is attributed or used for bidding.

## 4. The customer's use case beats the company label

**What happened:** paying customers used the same data for different jobs: choosing which events to attend, researching companies, shortlisting target accounts. Some did not need contact enrichment at all; some bought on their own and declined interviews.

**Method:** record the customer's industry, whom they sell to, the event's industry and the buying job as separate fields. Accept unknowns; stop interview requests after a clear no. Include lost and churned customers in research to get the denominator right.

**Do not generalize:** two customers sharing a job is a reason to test, not a conversion rate for an industry. A large-company email domain is not a group contract or permission to show a logo.

## 5. Billing rules build or break trust

**What happened:** a customer reported being charged credits for searches that returned nothing. The team returned the credits and changed the rule.

**Method:** explain credit and failed-task rules on the sample, trial and pricing pages before purchase, and record honest feedback after the fix. Keep promises within what product, sales and support can deliver together.

**Do not generalize:** a renewal that followed the fix does not prove the fix caused it.

## 6. AI's value shows up in work you can check

**What happened:** the working setup combined AI research, first drafts, human review and result summaries, with specific approval for anything external. A report saved to disk did not mean anything was executed or that anyone was notified.

**Method:** make the manual process clear first, then let AI shorten evidence gathering and production. Keep sources, product facts, versions and execution receipts.

## 7. Old plans need correcting, not just archiving

**What happened:** an older competitive strategy jumped from estimated traffic to channel contribution, from company size to "the competitor cannot serve this buyer", and from event counts to an imagined ROI ranking.

**Method:** keep the research directions, drop the certainty. A competitor's real channel efficiency, customer preferences and ROI need their own evidence. Having been written into a strategy document does not make something proven.

## Moving experience between products

Experience from consumer products (creator partnerships, launch platforms, deal sites, video) transfers as process: production, listing, partnership and review routines. The buyer, deal size, usage frequency and purchasing path still have to be validated from scratch.
