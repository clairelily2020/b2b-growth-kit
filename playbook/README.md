# B2B growth playbook

From the first real need to customers you can win and keep again and again.

Written for early B2B teams selling internationally and for the first marketing lead. The default setting is one or two marketers, with product, engineering and sales joining as needed. It does not ask you to build a full marketing department first, and it does not count the number of channels as progress.

**Find where you are stuck first, then open that chapter.** Stages are not set by company age, funding round or signup count. A product can have large deals and still no repeatable way to win customers.

| The problem in front of you | Start here | What this stage should produce |
|---|---|---|
| Not sure who buys or why now | [S0 Validation](stages/00-validation.md) | A buying job, counter-evidence, a pilot you can sell |
| A product, but no first paying customers | [S1 First customers](stages/01-first-customers.md) | A deliverable offer, real usage and payment records |
| Scattered deals; every one feels like starting over | [S2 Repeatability](stages/02-repeatability.md) | A repeatable path for one segment, with costs recorded |
| Signups grow, but usage and renewals lag | [S3 Retention and expansion](stages/03-retention.md) | First value, reasons to keep using, customer proof |
| Repeatability exists; ready to invest more | [S4 Scale and team](stages/04-scale.md) | Affordable acquisition cost, handoffs, a capacity plan |

## Status of the advice

Everything here is one of three kinds:

- **Practice record:** backed by real reports or records, possibly with unchecked items.
- **Working suggestion:** a method designed for small teams, not an industry standard.
- **Hypothesis:** worth testing, without commercial results of its own yet.

Inspired in part by the [B2B Playbook by Ivan Xu](https://github.com/weilun88313/B2B-Playbook). This playbook uses its own stage structure and original templates; it does not copy that project's text, templates or resource ratings.
