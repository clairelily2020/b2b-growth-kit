# Content: produce around the buyer's job, then plan distribution

Collect what buyers say while evaluating and using the product, then pick topics. Replace "we need three posts a week" with "which question is the buyer stuck on this week, and what material helps them move forward".

## Content by buying stage

| What the buyer is doing | Format | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Recognizing the problem | Founder opinion, a mistake reviewed, research findings | Read one concrete method |
| Comparing approaches | Alternatives, workflow comparisons, cost explanations | See a sample or a task demo |
| Evaluating vendors | Product demo, case study, data explanation, FAQ | Trial or a scoped pilot |
| Selling internally | One-page proposal, procurement Q&A, pilot results | Talk with the budget or procurement owner |
| Starting to use | Task tutorials, checklists, troubleshooting | Reach first value |

## One piece of research, many uses

After a sourced analysis of a buyer problem: write the main document; record a task demo; pull a judgment that works on LinkedIn; give a partner newsletter its own angle; add buyers' follow-up questions to the FAQ. Every rewrite keeps the same factual limits; no result gets added for the sake of a short video.

Decide distribution before writing: who will share it, which community has this problem, which partner can use it, and when sales will send it to a prospect. Early on, a long white paper with no distribution path is usually worth less than one useful sample page.

## Video choices

A product demo shows the job and the result first, then the clicks. Screen recordings prove a real flow; animation explains abstract ideas; customer video proves experience. Generated imagery can illustrate a point; it must not fake customers, interviews or product capability.

AI video tools speed up production, but choose the question and the channel first, then decide whether complex animation is worth it. Reusing existing recordings usually costs far less than a new brand film.

## Writing acceptance

Remove unsupported superlatives, vague efficiency promises and forced lists of three. Each paragraph should give the buyer one more actionable piece of information. Source links point to the page that supports that exact sentence; include dates and product versions when they affect the conclusion.
