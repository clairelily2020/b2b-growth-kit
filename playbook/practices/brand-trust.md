# Brand and trust from zero

An early brand answers three buyer questions: who are you, why should I listen, and can you do what you say. Visual consistency helps, but it never replaces a product and delivery the buyer can check.

## Before you have customer stories

Show the real product in use, where sample data comes from, when it was updated, what it applies to and its limits. Explain a problem buyers keep running into, publicly, with a method they can check. Demo cases, method write-ups and internal tests are fine when labelled as such; never present simulated results as customer outcomes.

The founder's background can explain why they understand the problem; it does not prove a new product works. Signup numbers from a past consumer product say nothing about B2B purchase value.

## Once you have first customers

Use the [proof card](../templates/proof-card.md) to record: the work before buying, the scope of use, the observation period, the result, the cost, and exactly what the customer allows you to publish. "Saved us a lot of time" and a timed saving are different kinds of evidence.

Get short, accurate feedback first; long case studies later. Logos, personal quotes, video and data each need their own permission. Paying is not consenting to endorse.

## Being remembered

Pick a few themes tied to the buyer's job that you can explain for years. A sales-intelligence company might own "how to pick the accounts worth pursuing before an event" and expand into list quality, handoff and post-event review. Do not switch to a new category slogan every week.

Keep the product name, description, colours and main demo path fixed, so the website, social profiles, sales decks and partner content say the same thing. Founder content carries judgment and reasoning; the company account carries product facts, updates and shareable material.

## Borrowing third-party trust

Niche advisers can validate a method; customers can vouch for the experience; media can bring visibility; review platforms can provide independent opinions. Sponsored content is labelled as sponsored and never posed as independent coverage. A paid membership or directory listing is not an endorsement by the association.

Without customer results, joint education is often more credible than paying someone to say the tool is great. A workshop built on a real problem leaves tutorials, recordings and Q&A that sales can reuse; still check that the audience was the right buyer.

## How to tell if it works

Watch for repeat visits from target accounts, branded search, self-reported source, material forwarded to colleagues, and trust objections in sales calls. Treat these as signals; do not convert them into brand revenue. Brand effects cross channels, and last-click reports cannot show them.

At each review ask: what do buyers understand faster now, which doubts come up less, and are there real referrals or smoother evaluations? Follower counts can be logged; on their own they do not prove trust.
