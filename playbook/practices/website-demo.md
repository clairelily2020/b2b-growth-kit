# Website, landing pages and demos

Different intents need different destinations. A buyer looking for suppliers and a sales team looking for pre-event targets may search for the same event, and still should not land on the same vague promise.

## What a page needs

1. Who it is for and which job, so the first screen answers "what can I get done here".
2. A sample, product screenshots or a real result, so the promise can be checked.
3. The steps, the inputs needed, and the data or service limits.
4. Real proof; without customer proof, clearly labelled demo material.
5. Answers on price, scope, updates, support and risk.
6. One main next step, and after submitting, who will do what and when.

"Free sample", "sign up" and "book a demo" are different levels of commitment. Choose by how you deliver, not by which one inflates the conversion rate.

## Launch check

Walk the normal path: ad or content link, page, CTA, signup or booking, confirmation page, CRM. Check mobile, load time, error messages, UTM persistence and owner notifications. Mark test data and keep it out of results.

Comparison pages cite competitors' current public pages, describe when each option fits and how they differ, and never invent features or pose as an independent review. Prices, payment records and source details stay out of public URLs.

## Running a demo

First confirm the job they need done, their current method and how they will judge. Demo one flow that matches their task: show the input and the result, then the limits. Midway, ask whether the result would fit their workflow, not just "does that make sense".

End with a clear next step: who provides input, who evaluates, when you review, whether procurement needs to join. Send a short recap afterwards. "We did a demo" is not pipeline progress.

## Optimizing with low traffic

Fix obvious breakage and missing information first, then watch real user sessions or run task tests. Changing audience, offer and page at once is a new-path test, not a single-variable A/B test. Without enough sample, keep a qualitative judgment and do not report invented uplift percentages.
