# Original research, data reports and PR

**Fits:** S2-S4; you have unique data that can be publicly checked.

**Before you start:** confirm data rights, sample scope, method and the reader's question. Without outcome data, do not publish a "highest ROI" ranking.

**How to run it:** choose a question buyers are actually deciding; explain data coverage, collection time, calculation method and limits. Produce reviewable findings, charts and explanations; offer newsworthy angles to relevant media; correct errors after publishing.

**Materials:** research method, data dictionary, verification notes, charts, claim review and media angles.

**How to judge:** pickup by target media, reading by target accounts, and reuse in sales. Do not promise that each story produces deals.

**Adjust or stop:** narrow the conclusion when a small sample is dressed up as an industry finding, the analysis cannot be recomputed, or sources cannot be disclosed. Keep sponsored articles and editorial coverage apart.

**Field note:** a directory of events and company counts cannot tell you anyone's return on exhibiting; a "best events" ranking built on it stays a hypothesis.
